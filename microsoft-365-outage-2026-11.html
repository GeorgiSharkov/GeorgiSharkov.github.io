Outage ≠ confirmed breach

The reviewed Microsoft update reports no evidence of malicious activity. A final root-cause report was still pending. Avoid presenting this disruption as a confirmed cyberattack or data theft. [1]

01 / WHAT HAPPENED

A shared dependency, uneven impact.

Reports of the disruption began on 12 November. Microsoft’s public dashboard still showed degradation when checked on 13 November. [1, 4]

Selected impact from Microsoft’s 10:01 UTC update on 13 November [1]
AreaReported impact

TeamsExchange-dependent features affected; most core calling, meetings and messaging remained functional.
Copilot / SharePointWork-data and dependent features affected; direct SharePoint file access remained functional.

The service list is selective, not a claim that every feature or tenant is affected. Use the provider’s latest scope statement before making operational decisions. A failed portal request is not sufficient evidence of a failed detection engine.

Find the record for your organisation

  1. Ask an authorised administrator to open Health → Service health in the Microsoft 365 admin center.
  2. Look for MO1550821; confirm which incident record applies to your tenant. [4]
  3. Read the latest update, affected features, timestamp and next update. If resolved, check Issue history.
  4. If you cannot reach the admin center, use the public status page. If impact is missing from your tenant’s records, use Report an issue or support. [2]

Microsoft’s documentation lists Service Support and Helpdesk administrator access among the roles that can view Service Health. Ordinary users can ask IT for the tenant-specific update. [2]

03 / PRACTICAL SOC ACTIONS

Confirm impact without losing visibility.

Original CyberShark guidance. These checks are recommendations, not findings about your environment.

01 / SCOPE

Record the disruption.

  1. Capture first observed failure in UTC, tenant, service and affected operation.
  2. Save the provider incident reference and most recent update time.
  3. Track business impact separately from a suspected security incident.
02 / VERIFY

Check your evidence path.

  1. Separate portal access from telemetry ingestion and alert delivery.
  2. Inspect ingestion freshness, connector health and queues using available tools.
  3. Document any visibility gap and plan a retrospective review after recovery.
03 / CONTINUE

Use approved fallbacks.

  1. Move urgent coordination to your agreed alternative channel.
  2. Preserve unsent communications and record delayed customer notifications.
  3. Do not disable MFA, protective policies or audit collection to work around access failures.
04 / RECOVER

Verify the return.

  1. Check essential functions with a small, authorised test.
  2. Review delayed alerts and communications before clearing the backlog.
  3. Close the operational incident with evidence and follow the provider’s final report.
Keep investigating

A provider outage does not explain every suspicious event. Continue reviewing unusual sign-ins, privilege changes and independent compromise evidence. Treat unsolicited “Microsoft support” instructions as unverified, and use established support channels.
