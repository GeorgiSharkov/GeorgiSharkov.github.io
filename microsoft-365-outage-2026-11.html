LATEST UPDATE / 15 NOVEMBER

From restoration to review.

This revision replaces the 13 November recovery snapshot. Original publication and update dates are retained.

Preliminary cause and restoration

Microsoft’s incident communication, republished in Penn’s administrative-console extract, says an earlier network-capacity upgrade left an issue exposed by a later network change. Connection requests then queued until affected servers exhausted processing capacity. This is a preliminary explanation, not a substitute for the final Post-Incident Report. [5]

  • 12 November: broad connectivity disruption reported. [4]
  • 13 November: this article’s original snapshot captured partial recovery. [1]
  • 14 November: Microsoft confirmed restoration to the remaining affected customers and regions, as reported by Sky News. [6]
  • 15 November: Microsoft’s public dashboard shows operational status at this review. [1]

Report follow-up: the incident communication committed to preliminary and final reports within two and five business days respectively. Check the tenant record for availability; the final report has not been independently reviewed here. [5]

Separate advisory

Penn’s extract still lists Teams Pro Management Portal device-monitoring advisory TM1551945. That narrower record should not be presented as the broad Microsoft 365 outage reopening. Confirm applicability and the latest status in your tenant. [5]

Find the record for your organisation

  1. Ask an authorised administrator to open Health → Service health in the Microsoft 365 admin center.
  2. Look in Issue history for MO1550821; confirm the record and impact relevant to your tenant. [2, 5]
  3. Read the final status, tenant impact and available Post-Incident Report. Keep the provider’s incident end time separate from your own recovery time.
  4. If problems persist, compare current Service Health advisories, record fresh errors and timestamps, and use Report an issue or support. A green public banner does not resolve every tenant-specific issue. [2]

Microsoft’s documentation lists Service Support and Helpdesk administrator access among the roles that can view Service Health. Ordinary users can ask IT for the tenant-specific update. [2]

03 / PRACTICAL SOC ACTIONS

Confirm impact without losing visibility.

Original CyberShark guidance. These checks are recommendations, not findings about your environment.

01 / SCOPE

Record the disruption.

  1. Capture first observed failure in UTC, tenant, service and affected operation.
  2. Save the provider incident reference and most recent update time.
  3. Track business impact separately from a suspected security incident.
02 / VERIFY

Check your evidence path.

  1. Separate portal access from telemetry ingestion and alert delivery.
  2. Inspect ingestion freshness, connector health and queues using available tools.
  3. Document any visibility gap and plan a retrospective review after recovery.
03 / CONTINUE

Use approved fallbacks.

  1. Move urgent coordination to your agreed alternative channel.
  2. Preserve unsent communications and record delayed customer notifications.
  3. Do not disable MFA, protective policies or audit collection to work around access failures.
04 / RECOVER

Verify the return.

  1. Check essential functions with a small, authorised test.
  2. Review delayed alerts and communications before clearing the backlog.
  3. Close the operational incident with evidence and follow the provider’s final report.
Keep investigating

A provider outage does not explain every suspicious event. Continue reviewing unusual sign-ins, privilege changes and independent compromise evidence. Treat unsolicited “Microsoft support” instructions as unverified, and use established support channels.
