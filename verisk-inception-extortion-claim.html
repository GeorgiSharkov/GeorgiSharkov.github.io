EXTORTION WATCH / 27 NOVEMBER 2026

Verisk and INCEPTION.One public claim. Four domains to hunt.

On 26 November, INCEPTION named Verisk on its extortion site. Several trackers saw the same post. That establishes who made the allegation; it does not establish who accessed a system or whose records were taken.

Review the indicators

EVIDENCE STATUS

Claim observed. Breach unverified.

As of 27 November, this review found one distinct public claim naming Verisk, no Verisk confirmation of data theft and no evidence establishing encryption or payment.

01 / CLAIM REGISTER

What the group alleges

Brandefense recorded a single INCEPTION listing. The group described 1.01 TB in 67,672 files, including 4,062 tables and 854.6 million rows. It claimed 17.5 million insurance contracts, 6.91 million de-identified US locations and more than 458 modelled-loss analyses. Those are different units: none is a verified count of affected people.

The listing also described treaty books, event catalogues, binary loss files and data across 176 tenant environments. It demanded contact within 24 hours and threatened publication on 27 November. The deadline alone establishes neither a release nor a ransom payment. RansomLook and ransomware.live sightings refer to this same demand, not additional attacks.

If the insurance and modelling files are authentic, they could reveal commercially sensitive portfolio exposures. The posting does not establish an outage, a successful login or an affected customer. Suppliers should verify their own exposure through an established Verisk contact.

02 / REPORTED INDICATORS

Four legal-themed lookalikes

CyPro reports these domains appeared in a threat feed on 24 November. Threadlinqs categorises them as inferred campaign infrastructure, without direct evidence of use against Verisk. Treat them as retrospective search terms. No incident-specific malware hash or source IP was verified for this article.

  • law-verisk[.]com

03 / SOC TRIAGE

Trace the user session before escalating

Search DNS, proxy and endpoint network records from 24 November for the exact hostnames and their subdomains. Restore the dots in your search tool. Record the UTC time, device, user and whether the connection was blocked. A security scanner resolving a domain has a different implication from a user opening it after a purported legal-team call.

For a user visit, check identity logs for unfamiliar sessions, new authentication methods and unusual SaaS downloads immediately afterwards. If you confirm credential disclosure or session misuse, revoke the affected sessions and preserve the identity and access logs. Avoid blocking legitimate Verisk services or shared hosting IPs because of a brand match.
